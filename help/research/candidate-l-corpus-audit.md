# Candidate L and L2 corpus audit

## Scope and provenance

This audit treats the rejected local-ranking candidates as research inputs, not
as production changes. No candidate code was merged or pushed.

- baseline production checkpoint: `36043991cb58d1332865ba9bc31d1cd988592c79`
  (descended from accepted checkpoint `ac7522293e305e62978e3f24e3a5d4ac9ae72f2d`)
- Candidate L scorer commit: `1fbf314699e5a3419fe3fd00492f924cf8439a5f`
- Candidate L audit harness commit: `6361200fdceac2ed9a078bbdcd9c80b045d3b462`
- Candidate L2 scorer commit: `c66af81022bcd1178f1ad000d066c00cf93e4164`
- Candidate L2 audit harness commit: `9f550281f5cf84c3e5d2f49f1a21d2e31fc7a4c8`
- runtime: Node `24.16.0`, pnpm `11.8.0`, Apple Silicon macOS
- settings: rotations and mirroring enabled; reorder `4`; beam `8`; fanout `4`;
  transform cap `8`; local repair disabled; edge-contact policy
- sheets: `1000x1300`, `1000x1700`, `2000x1700`, `2000x2700`
- exact raw reports: [raw](../artifacts/candidate-l-audit/raw/)

The audit harness runs the same prepared pieces on all four sheets and compares
canonical collision geometry after ignoring translation, winding, ring origin,
piece order, and quarter turns. It preserves reflection and relative placement.

## Production verdict

Reject both candidates as production changes in their present form.

- Both fail the current triangle golden bounds, even though their triangle
  output is a coherent, connected, hole-free lattice rather than a chaotic
  failure.
- Candidate L improves sheet stability substantially, but regresses the
  approved `2000x2700` mixed61 area by `19.84%`.
- Candidate L2 is not stable across sheets and regresses that same reference by
  `34.80%`.
- Candidate L2's raw-contact-first local order removes most of Candidate L's
  useful invariance signal.

This is a shipping rejection, not a rejection of the underlying ideas.

## Research verdict

Retain Candidate L as a strong research branch. Its combination of structural
contact, normalized contact bands, and intrinsic envelope criteria reduced the
mixed61 area spread across four sheets from `276459.435 mm2` to
`50457.263 mm2`, an `81.75%` reduction. It also improved three non-reference
mixed61 sheets by `18.57%` to `24.86%` and made the homogeneous rectangle case
exactly sheet-invariant.

The most promising next experiment is to reuse Candidate L's intrinsic local
ranking while protecting the triangle lattice and other valuable branches with
explicit beam diversity or a different decoder/terminal selector. A triangle
golden failure means the candidate cannot ship unchanged; it does not prove
that its ranking ingredients are useless.

Candidate L2 is a weaker research lead. It confirms that raw shared millimeters
must not replace structural/contact-band information as the first local key.

### Mixed50

Candidate L is mixed rather than a clear win:

- `1000x1700`: area `658162.610 mm2` (`+10.89%`), holes `7` instead of `10`
- `2000x2700`: area `616190.131 mm2` (`-1.13%`), holes `9` instead of `10`

Candidate L2 is worse on both reference comparisons:

- `1000x1700`: area `798566.294 mm2` (`+34.55%`), holes `5`
- `2000x2700`: area `641955.385 mm2` (`+3.00%`), holes `8`

The lower hole counts do not compensate for L2's much larger envelope. Portable
previews and full four-sheet metrics are under
[candidate-l-audit](../artifacts/candidate-l-audit/).

## Recommended recombination gate

Do not require a research branch to pass the triangle golden before it can be
measured. Require the golden only before production acceptance. For research:

1. preserve every candidate implementation and exact harness commit;
2. measure triangle structure, mixed61 sheet spread, mixed50, and a homogeneous
   case independently;
3. retain ingredients that improve a gate materially;
4. recombine them in isolated worktrees;
5. require the full triangle golden and corpus gates only for the final shipping
   candidate.

For L specifically, the next gate should test an intrinsic local-ranking lane
alongside a protected structural-contact lane in the same beam. That directly
tests whether diversity can keep the triangle motif without giving up L's
sheet-stability advantage.
